Know what you are buying

The five types of SEO provider, compared for Denver buyers

Before comparing individual firms, compare the categories. A freelancer, a boutique, a full-service agency, a national franchise and an in-house hire solve the same problem with different trade-offs, and most bad fits happen because the buyer picked the wrong category, not the wrong firm.

Updated October 2026 6 minute read Denver, Colorado

This page deliberately names no competitors. Rankings of "top Denver SEO companies" published by third parties are almost all either paid placements or scraped from review counts, and a list written by an agency would be worth even less. What a buyer can usefully compare is structure: who does the work, who you talk to, what it costs, and what happens when something goes wrong. Those follow from the type of company far more reliably than from its marketing.

Freelancers and independent consultants

Denver has a large pool of independent SEO practitioners, many of whom left agencies or tech companies along the Front Range. The best are excellent value: you are buying senior judgement without the overhead of a sales team. The risk is capacity. One person cannot do technical work, write content, manage a Business Profile and build links at volume, so a freelancer either specializes or stretches thin. Vacations, illness and a larger client landing in the same month will all show up in your results.

Hire a freelancer when your budget is below roughly $2,000 a month, you have one location, and you or someone on your staff can handle implementation on the site. Verify what the freelancer does themselves versus subcontracts; some "freelancers" are resellers of offshore work. And insist on every account being in your name, because a sole trader who disappears takes the logins with them.

Boutique agencies

A boutique is a small firm, usually between three and fifteen people, often founder-led, with a focus on one or two disciplines. In Denver these tend to cluster around local search for service businesses, content for professional services, or technical work for e-commerce. Their pitch is senior attention at a price between a freelancer and a full-service firm, and when it is true it is the sweet spot for most owner-operated businesses in the metro.

The questions that matter: is the founder still involved in client work or only in sales, how many accounts does each strategist carry, and what happens when the firm wins a client twice your size. Boutiques tend to score well on ownership, contract terms and communication on the vetting scorecard, and less consistently on technical depth, which they often subcontract.

Full-service agencies

Full-service firms offer SEO alongside paid media, web design, social, PR and sometimes traditional advertising. Denver has a healthy number of them, from long-established shops in LoDo and RiNo to newer firms built around a particular industry. They suit businesses that need several channels coordinated by one team and that have the budget to be a meaningful account.

The trade-off is layers. You will have an account manager, and the strategist, the writer and the developer will be people you rarely meet. SEO can also be the least profitable service line in a full-service shop and get the least senior staff. Ask specifically how many dedicated SEO people the firm has, who leads the discipline, and whether you can meet them. Watch for proprietary CMS platforms, which are common in agencies that also build websites and which complicate leaving.

National franchises and marketing platforms

Several national SEO and "digital marketing for small business" brands sell into Denver through local franchisees or sales reps, with fulfilment done centrally. The appeal is a predictable, low-involvement package at a modest price, and for some owners that is exactly right.

The structural problems are consistent: the person who sold you the service has no control over the work, the playbook is the same in Denver as in Tampa, local market nuance is absent, and the website and listings are frequently hosted on the franchise platform under the franchise's account. Contracts are typically 12 months with automatic renewal. If you go this route, treat ownership and termination as non-negotiable, read the contract red flags page with the agreement in hand, and expect to score the firm low on local proof and communication cadence.

Hiring in-house

An in-house SEO specialist in Denver costs somewhere between $65,000 and $110,000 in salary depending on seniority, which with benefits and tools puts the real monthly figure around $5,500 to $9,500. For that you get someone who learns your business deeply, is available every day, and whose incentives are entirely aligned with yours.

It makes sense when you have enough work to fill a week, every week: a multi-location business, a large content operation, or an e-commerce site with constant changes. It makes less sense for a single-location service business, where the work is lumpy and a generalist marketer will spend most of the week on other things. A common hybrid is an in-house marketing manager who owns the strategy and uses a freelancer or boutique for execution and specialist advice.

Choosing a category before choosing a firm

  1. Budget under $1,500 a month: a freelancer, or a boutique's starter tier. Avoid franchises unless you have read the contract carefully.
  2. $1,500–$5,000 a month, one to three locations: a boutique, or a full-service firm with a dedicated SEO team. Compare one of each.
  3. Over $5,000 a month, or several channels: a full-service agency, or an in-house lead with specialist support.
  4. Large site or multi-state footprint: in-house plus a technical specialist, or an agency with demonstrable enterprise experience.

Whatever category you land on, the firms inside it still vary enormously. Shortlist at least two from the category you think fits and one from the adjacent category, run them through the 25 questions, and score all three on the interactive scorecard. Check the quotes against the pricing benchmarks so that a low score is not excused by a low price.
